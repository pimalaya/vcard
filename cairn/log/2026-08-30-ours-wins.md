# Ours wins, and the preference is gone

`prefer` was argued as a separation of concerns: the baseline side is a question about bytes, the winning side a question about policy, and answering the second by the first would let a byte-fidelity choice settle a data-loss one. The argument reads well and no caller ever drew the distinction. tCard and neverest both passed `Left`; nothing in the ecosystem passed `Right`. A caller reaches for a merge holding the version it is merging into, and that version is both the one it would rather not churn and the one it means to keep. Git already names the arrangement, so the left side is `ours` and wins, the right side is `theirs`, and neither is stated at a call site any more. ical-rs landed the same change the same day.

A mechanism died quietly with it. `Merger::replaces` answered whether a right-side action a left one collided with still lands, as `!scraps && prefer == Right`, and with the preference gone it is unconditionally false. Every branch behind it was unreachable: the parameter an addition beat being replaced where it stood, the parameter an update beat being found by its key rather than by its base value, and the left-side line a winning addition removed. `param_position` and `VcardMergeAction::is_removal` had no other caller and went with them. Eight collision sites collapsed to recording the conflict and returning, which is what every reachable path already did.

Two guards survived on purpose, because neither ever consulted the preference. A left-side removal met by a right-side edit still restores the whole right line through `readded` in `apply_pair`, and a left-side parameter removal met by a right-side `ParamChanged` or `ParamAdded` still lands through `restore` and `removed`. An update beats a removal whichever side it came from, which was never the caller's to invert, and `an_update_still_beats_a_removal` and `an_update_beats_a_parameter_removal_on_either_side` hold it down.

Five tests went with the preference. Four existed only to show it could be flipped: one carrying the right side's value on a plain divergence, one carrying it on an identity-keyed edit, one replacing a beaten parameter and one replacing a beaten addition. The fifth asserted that stating `Left` gave what saying nothing gave. Every law behind the four already had a left-first twin in the same module, `divergent_edits_conflict_and_the_left_wins`, `divergent_edits_of_an_identity_keyed_property_conflict`, `divergent_parameter_changes_conflict_and_the_left_wins` and `divergent_additions_of_a_single_valued_property_conflict`, so nothing had to be restated. A sixth kept its assertions and stopped naming the preference, and three that looped over both now run once. The property test asserting the preference changed no report is gone with what it compared.

Capabilities moved: merge.
